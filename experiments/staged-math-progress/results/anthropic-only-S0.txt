# S0: Task parsing and claim boundary

## Task Identity

**Experiment:** SV-COST-STAGED-001
**Goal:** Characterize the ALLOW admissibility predicate for an *abstract* GCAT/BCAT evaluator defined solely by the stipulation below.

## Stipulated Abstract Evaluator

Let the universe of discourse be a set **T** of *candidate transitions*. Three predicates are given by stipulation (not derived from any deployed engine):

| Symbol | Reading |
|--------|---------|
| `GBP : T → Prop` | "Good Basis Property holds" |
| `BD : T → Prop` | "Boundary Disposition holds" |
| `OC : T → Prop` | "Operational Condition holds" |

**Stipulated rule:**

> `ALLOW(t)` iff `GBP(t) ∧ BD(t) ∧ OC(t)`

This is a *definition by stipulation*; it has no additional hidden clauses.

## Claim Boundary

**What is claimed (in-scope):**

1. The necessary-and-sufficient characterization of `ALLOW` follows tautologically from the stipulation: `ALLOW(t) ↔ GBP(t) ∧ BD(t) ∧ OC(t)`.
2. A Lean 4 statement encoding this biconditional (forthcoming in a later stage) will be a purely logical artifact about the abstract predicates.

**What is NOT claimed (out-of-scope):**

- No claim is made about any deployed GCAT/BCAT engine, implementation, or runtime.
- The predicates `GBP`, `BD`, `OC` are *uninterpreted*; no binding to concrete system behavior is asserted or implied.
- Proof of correctness of any software system is outside scope.

## Unresolved Implementation Binding

The mapping from `{GBP, BD, OC}` to any concrete evaluator internals is **explicitly unresolved and out of scope**. Any such binding would require a separate, empirically grounded specification artifact not present here.
