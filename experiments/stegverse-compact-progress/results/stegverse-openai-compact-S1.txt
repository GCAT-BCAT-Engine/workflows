S1: Definitions and assumptions.

### Stipulated abstract model

Let `Transition` be an arbitrary type of candidate transitions. Let

- `GBP : Transition → Prop`,
- `BD : Transition → Prop`,
- `OC : Transition → Prop`

be predicates on candidate transitions.

Define abstract admissibility by

\[
\operatorname{ALLOW}(t) \;:\!\iff\; GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t).
\]

No further properties of `Transition`, `GBP`, `BD`, or `OC` are assumed.

### Lean 4 definition candidate

```lean
universe u

variable {Transition : Type u}

variable (GBP BD OC : Transition → Prop)

def ALLOW (t : Transition) : Prop :=
  GBP t ∧ BD t ∧ OC t
```

This is an unverified logical-core candidate, not evidence about any concrete implementation.

### Generated claim target

For every `t : Transition`, the intended necessary-and-sufficient characterization is

\[
\operatorname{ALLOW}(t)\iff GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t).
\]

Its two directions are:

- Necessity: `ALLOW t → GBP t ∧ BD t ∧ OC t`.
- Sufficiency: `GBP t ∧ BD t ∧ OC t → ALLOW t`.

No proof or Lean verification is asserted at this stage.

### Claim boundary

The definitions concern only the stipulated abstract evaluator. They do not establish that any deployed or concrete GCAT/BCAT implementation computes these predicates correctly, applies this decision rule, or conforms to the characterization.
