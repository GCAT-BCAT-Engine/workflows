S4: Complete informal proof.

### Stipulation

Let `Transition` be an abstract type, and let

- `GBP : Transition → Prop`
- `BD : Transition → Prop`
- `OC : Transition → Prop`
- `ALLOW : Transition → Prop`

Assume the evaluator specification

\[
hEval:\forall t,\quad ALLOW(t)\leftrightarrow
\bigl(GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t)\bigr).
\]

No decidability, independence, or interpretation of these predicates is assumed.

### Generated claim

For every candidate transition \(t\),

\[
ALLOW(t)\iff GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t).
\]

Equivalently:

1. **Necessity**
   \[
   ALLOW(t)\to GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t).
   \]

2. **Sufficiency**
   \[
   GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t)\to ALLOW(t).
   \]

### Complete informal proof

Fix an arbitrary transition \(t\).

For necessity, suppose \(ALLOW(t)\) holds. By the forward direction of the stipulated evaluator equivalence \(hEval(t)\), it follows that \(GBP(t)\), \(BD(t)\), and \(OC(t)\) all hold. Hence

\[
GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t).
\]

For sufficiency, suppose

\[
GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t).
\]

By the reverse direction of \(hEval(t)\), this conjunction implies \(ALLOW(t)\).

Thus both implications hold, so

\[
ALLOW(t)\iff GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t).
\]

Because \(t\) was arbitrary, the characterization holds for every candidate transition.
