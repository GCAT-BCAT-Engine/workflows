**Stipulated setting**
Let `Transition` be an arbitrary type, with predicates:

- `GBP : Transition → Prop`
- `BD : Transition → Prop`
- `OC : Transition → Prop`
- `ALLOW : Transition → Prop`

Assume the abstract evaluator specification:

`∀ t : Transition, ALLOW(t) ↔ (GBP(t) ∧ BD(t) ∧ OC(t))`.

**Theorem formulation — exact admissibility characterization**
For every candidate transition `t`:

`ALLOW(t) ↔ (GBP(t) ∧ BD(t) ∧ OC(t))`.

Equivalently, admissibility has both directions:

1. **Necessity**

   `ALLOW(t) → GBP(t) ∧ BD(t) ∧ OC(t)`.

2. **Sufficiency**

   `GBP(t) ∧ BD(t) ∧ OC(t) → ALLOW(t)`.

Thus, under the stipulated abstract decision rule, a transition is allowed if and only if all three predicates hold.
