**Stipulated context**
- `Transition` is an unspecified type.
- `GBP`, `BD`, `OC`, and `ALLOW` have type `Transition → Prop`.
- The evaluator specification is:
  \[
  \forall t:\mathrm{Transition},\quad

**Proof strategy**

Fix an arbitrary transition \(t\), then specialize the evaluator specification at \(t\):
\[
ALLOW(t)\iff GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t).
\]

Use its two directions separately:

1. Apply the forward implication to derive necessity:
   \[
   ALLOW(t)\to GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t).
   \]

2. Apply the reverse implication to derive sufficiency:
   \[
   GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t)\to ALLOW(t).
   \]

3. Combine those implications with biconditional introduction to recover the characterization:
   \[
   ALLOW(t)\iff GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t).
   \]

Because \(t\) is arbitrary, generalize each result over all transitions.

**Lemma decomposition**

1. **Necessity lemma**
   \[
   \forall t,\quad ALLOW(t)\to GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t).
   \]

2. **Optional component projections**
   \[
   \forall t,\quad ALLOW(t)\to GBP(t),
   \]
   \[
   \forall t,\quad ALLOW(t)\to BD(t),
   \]
   \[
   \forall t,\quad ALLOW(t)\to OC(t).
   \]
   These follow by projecting the conjunction produced by the necessity lemma. They are not required to prove the main equivalence.

3. **Sufficiency lemma**
   \[
   \forall t,\quad GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t)\to ALLOW(t).
   \]

4. **Characterization theorem**
   \[
   \forall t,\quad ALLOW(t)\iff GBP(t)\land BD(t)\land OC(t).
   \]
   This follows by combining the necessity and sufficiency lemmas. It also follows directly from the stipulated evaluator specification.
