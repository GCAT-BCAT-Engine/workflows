### Stipulations
Let:
- `Transition` and `Outcome` be arbitrary types;
- `ALLOW : Outcome`;
- `eval : Transition → Outcome`;
- `GBP BD OC : Transition → Prop`;
- `Allowed t` abbreviate `eval t = ALLOW`;
- `Conditions t` abbreviate `GBP t ∧ (BD t ∧ OC t)`.

Assume the abstract evaluator specification:
\[
\forall t:\mathrm{Transition},\quad
eval(t)=ALLOW\leftrightarrow GBP(t)\land(BD(t)\land OC(t)).
\]

### Necessary-and-sufficient characterization
For every candidate transition \(t\),
\[
eval(t)=ALLOW
\quad\leftrightarrow\quad
GBP(t)\land(BD(t)\land OC(t)).
\]

Thus all three predicates are jointly:

- **necessary** for `ALLOW`, and
- **sufficient** for `ALLOW`.

### Complete informal proof
Fix an arbitrary candidate transition \(t\).

For necessity, suppose \(eval(t)=ALLOW\). Instantiating the stipulated evaluator specification at \(t\) gives:
\[
eval(t)=ALLOW\leftrightarrow GBP(t)\land(BD(t)\land OC(t)).
\]
Applying the forward direction of this biconditional to the supposition yields:
\[
GBP(t)\land(BD(t)\land OC(t)).
\]
Therefore `GBP t`, `BD t`, and `OC t` all hold whenever the evaluator returns `ALLOW`.

For sufficiency, suppose:
\[
GBP(t)\land(BD(t)\land OC(t)).
\]
Apply the reverse direction of the same instantiated evaluator specification. It yields:
\[
eval(t)=ALLOW.
\]
Therefore the conjunction of `GBP t`, `BD t`, and `OC t` is sufficient for the evaluator to return `ALLOW`.

Both implications have now been established, so:
\[
eval(t)=ALLOW
\leftrightarrow
GBP(t)\land(BD(t)\land OC(t)).
\]
Because \(t\) was arbitrary, this characterization holds for every candidate transition.
