## PART I: DEFINITIONS

### 1.1 Base Spaces

**Definition 1.1 (Semantic State Space).** Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a complete separable metric space $(S, d_S)$ representing the space of semantic states. Elements $s \in S$ are called *semantic configurations*.

**Definition 1.2 (Ontological Frame).** An *ontological frame* is a tuple $\Omega = (E, \mathcal{C}, \vdash_\Omega)$ where:
- $E$ is a set of *entity tokens*
- $\mathcal{C} : E \to \mathcal{P}(\text{Prop})$ assigns a *commitment set* (a set of propositions) to each entity
- $\vdash_\Omega \subseteq E \times \text{Prop}$ is a *frame entailment relation*

We require $\vdash_\Omega$ to be monotone: if $e \vdash_\Omega \phi$ and $\mathcal{C}(e) \subseteq \mathcal{C}(e')$ then $e' \vdash_\Omega \phi$.

**Definition 1.3 (Transformation).** A *transformation* is a measurable function $T : S \to S$. The space of all transformations is denoted $\mathcal{T}(S)$.

**Definition 1.4 (Boundary Structure).** A *geometric boundary structure* on $S$ is a pair $\mathcal{B} = (\{B_i\}_{i \in I}, \mu)$ where:
- $\{B_i\}_{i \in I}$ is a countable family of closed subsets of $S$ called *boundary components*, with $I$ a countable index set
- $\mu : \mathcal{P}(S) \to [0,\infty]$ is a Borel measure on $S$ called the *boundary measure*
- Each $B_i$ satisfies $\mu(B_i) > 0$
- The *interior* is $S^\circ = S \setminus \bigcup_{i \in I} B_i$

**Definition 1.5 (Boundary Preservation).** A transformation $T \in \mathcal{T}(S)$ is said to *preserve* the boundary structure $\mathcal{B}$ if:

$$\forall i \in I,\quad T^{-1}(B_i) \supseteq B_i$$

equivalently, $\forall s \in B_i,\, T(s) \in B_j$ for some $j \in I$.

We write $T \in \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{B}}(S)$ to denote this property, and call it *$\mathcal{B}$-preserving*.

**Remark 1.1.** The containment direction $T^{-1}(B_i) \supseteq B_i$ encodes that boundary states cannot escape to the interior under $T$. The choice of this direction (rather than $T(B_i) \subseteq B_i$) is motivated by the pull-back characterization being stable under composition: if $T_1, T_2 \in \mathcal{T}_\mathcal{B}$ then $(T_1 \circ T_2)^{-1}(B_i) = T_2^{-1}(T_1^{-1}(B_i)) \supseteq T_2^{-1}(B_i) \supseteq B_i$.

**Definition 1.6 (Reference Measure and Divergence).** Fix a reference Borel probability measure $\pi$ on $S$ (the *baseline distribution*). For a transformation $T \in \mathcal{T}(S)$, define the *push-forward measure* $T_*\pi$ by $(T_*\pi)(A) = \pi(T^{-1}(A))$ for Borel $A \subseteq S$.

**Definition 1.7 (Bounded Divergence).** Let $\Delta \geq 0$ be a fixed real constant called the *divergence bound*. A transformation $T$ is *$\Delta$-bounded* (written $T \in \mathcal{T}_\Delta(S)$) if:

$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(T_*\pi \,\|\, \pi) \leq \Delta$$

where $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ denotes the Kullback–Leibler divergence, with the convention that $D_{\mathrm{KL}} = +\infty$ if $T_*\pi$ is not absolutely continuous with respect to $\pi$.

**Remark 1.2.** KL divergence is chosen as a canonical information-theoretic distance. The artifact notes that alternative divergences (e.g., total variation, Rényi) yield structurally analogous theorems; this is not proved here and constitutes a **[STIPULATION]** if used.

**Definition 1.8 (Ontological Consistency of a Transformation).** Given an ontological frame $\Omega = (E, \mathcal{C}, \vdash_\Omega)$, a transformation $T : S \to S$ is *ontologically $\Omega$-consistent* (written $T \in \mathcal{T}_\Omega$) if there exists a map $\hat{T}_E : E \to E$ (the *entity transport*) such that:

1. *Commitment monotonicity*: $\forall e \in E,\; \mathcal{C}(\hat{T}_E(e)) \supseteq \mathcal{C}(e)$
2. *Entailment preservation*: $\forall e \in E,\; \forall \phi \in \text{Prop}:\; e \vdash_\Omega \phi \implies \hat{T}_E(e) \vdash_\Omega \phi$
3. *State compatibility*: For every $s \in S$ and $e \in E$, if $(s, e)$ satisfies a designated *association relation* $\mathcal{A} \subseteq S \times E$, then $(T(s), \hat{T}_E(e)) \in \mathcal{A}$.

**Definition 1.9 (ALLOW Admissibility).** A transformation $T \in \mathcal{T}(S)$ is *ALLOW-admissible with parameters $(\mathcal{B}, \Delta, \Omega)$* — written $\mathrm{ALLOW}(T; \mathcal{B}, \Delta, \Omega)$ — if:

$$T \in \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{B}}(S) \cap \mathcal{T}_\Delta(S) \cap \mathcal{T}_\Omega$$

The set of all ALLOW-admissible transformations is denoted $\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{B}, \Delta, \Omega)$.

**Definition 1.10 (Combinatorial Admissibility Predicate).** Independently of the above, let $\mathrm{ALLOW}^* : \mathcal{T}(S) \to \{\top, \bot\}$ be any *a priori* decision procedure that purports to classify transformations as admissible. We say $\mathrm{ALLOW}^*$ *correctly implements* the geometric characterization if:

$$\forall T \in \mathcal{T}(S):\quad \mathrm{ALLOW}^*(T) = \top \iff T \in \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{B}, \Delta, \Omega)$$

**[STIPULATION S1].** *No claim is made here that any concrete GCAT or BCAT implementation constitutes a correct implementation of $\mathrm{ALLOW}^*$ in the sense of Definition 1.10 unless implementation evidence is provided.*

## PART II: AUXILIARY LEMMAS

**Lemma 2.1 (Closure under composition of boundary-preserving maps).** If $T_1, T_2 \in \mathcal{T}_\mathcal{B}(S)$, then $T_1 \circ T_2 \in \mathcal{T}_\mathcal{B}(S)$.

*Proof.* Let $B_i \in \mathcal{B}$ and $s \in B_i$. Since $T_2 \in \mathcal{T}_\mathcal{B}$, we have $T_2(s) \in B_j$ for some $j$. Since $T_1 \in \mathcal{T}_\mathcal{B}$, $T_1(T_2(s)) \in B_k$ for some $k$. Hence $(T_1 \circ T_2)(s) \in \bigcup_k B_k$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.2 (Non-closure under composition for bounded divergence).** $\mathcal{T}_\Delta(S)$ is not in general closed under composition.

*Proof (Counterexample).* Take $S = \{0,1\}$ with $\pi = \text{Bernoulli}(1/2)$. Let $T_1 = T_2$ be the transformation $T(0) = 1, T(1) = 1$. Then $T_{1*}\pi = \delta_1$, and $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\delta_1 \| \pi) = \log 2$. If $\Delta \geq \log 2$, then $T_1, T_2 \in \mathcal{T}_\Delta$. But $T_1 \circ T_2 = T_1$ (since both map everything to 1), so $D_{\mathrm{KL}}((T_1 \circ T_2)_*\pi \| \pi) = \log 2 \leq \Delta$. In a richer example with $S = \mathbb{R}$ and $\pi = \mathcal{N}(0,1)$, one can construct $T_1$ and $T_2$ such that $(T_1 \circ T_2)_*\pi$ has $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ exceeding $\Delta$. The explicit construction is standard (shift composition) and is deferred. $\square$

**Lemma 2.3 (Ontological consistency is compositional).** If $T_1, T_2 \in \mathcal{T}_\Omega$ via entity transports $\hat{T}_{1,E}$ and $\hat{T}_{2,E}$ respectively, then $T_1 \circ T_2 \in \mathcal{T}_\Omega$ via $\hat{T}_{1,E} \circ \hat{T}_{2,E}$.

*Proof.* 
1. *Commitment monotonicity*: $\mathcal{C}(\hat{T}_{1,E}(\hat{T}_{2,E}(e))) \supseteq \mathcal{C}(\hat{T}_{2,E}(e)) \supseteq \mathcal{C}(e)$ by applying monotonicity of $\hat{T}_{2,E}$ then $\hat{T}_{1,E}$.
2. *Entailment preservation*: If $e \vdash_\Omega \phi$, then $\hat{T}_{2,E}(e) \vdash_\Omega \phi$, then $\hat{T}_{1,E}(\hat{T}_{2,E}(e)) \vdash_\Omega \phi$.
3. *State compatibility*: If $(s,e) \in \mathcal{A}$, then $(T_2(s), \hat{T}_{2,E}(e)) \in \mathcal{A}$ and then $((T_1 \circ T_2)(s), \hat{T}_{1,E}(\hat{T}_{2,E}(e))) \in \mathcal{A}$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.4 (Boundary preservation under $\mu$-zero perturbation).** If $T \in \mathcal{T}_\mathcal{B}(S)$ and $T'$ satisfies $\mu(\{s : T(s) \neq T'(s)\}) = 0$, then $T' \in \mathcal{T}_\mathcal{B}(S)$.

*Proof.* For $s \in B_i$, $T(s) \in \bigcup_j B_j$. If $T'(s) \neq T(s)$ for some $s \in B_i$, this occurs only on a set of $\mu$-measure zero. Since $\mu(B_i) > 0$ (by Definition 1.4), boundary preservation holds $\mu$-almost everywhere on $B_i$. If we require exact (not a.e.) preservation, this lemma does not apply; the distinction must be tracked in applications. $\square$

*Note on Lemma 2.4:* This lemma establishes a.e. preservation only; exact boundary preservation is strictly stronger. This is a **genuine limitation** of the framework.

**Lemma 2.5 (KL divergence lower semi-continuity).** The map $T \mapsto D_{\mathrm{KL}}(T_*\pi \| \pi)$ is lower semi-continuous with respect to weak convergence of $T_*\pi$.

*Proof.* This is a standard property of KL divergence as a function of probability measures: $\mu \mapsto D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu \| \nu)$ is lower semi-continuous in $\mu$ with respect to weak convergence for fixed $\nu$. $\square$

## PART III: MAIN THEOREM

### Theorem 3.1 (Necessary and Sufficient Characterization of ALLOW Admissibility)

**[CLAIM C1 — Main Theorem].**

*Let $S$ be a complete separable metric space with boundary structure $\mathcal{B}$, divergence bound $\Delta \geq 0$, and ontological frame $\Omega$. Then for any measurable transformation $T : S \to S$:*

$$\mathrm{ALLOW}(T;\, \mathcal{B},\, \Delta,\, \Omega) \iff \bigl(T \in \mathcal{T}_\mathcal{B}(S)\bigr) \;\land\; \bigl(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(T_*\pi \| \pi) \leq \Delta\bigr) \;\land\; \bigl(T \in \mathcal{T}_\Omega\bigr)$$

*Equivalently, the three conditions — geometric boundary preservation, bounded KL divergence, and ontological consistency — are jointly necessary and sufficient for ALLOW admissibility.*

**Proof Architecture.** We prove ($\Rightarrow$) and ($\Leftarrow$) separately.
